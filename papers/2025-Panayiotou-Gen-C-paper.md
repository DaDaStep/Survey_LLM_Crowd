## Research Problem

Existing methods fall short of capturing the complex behaviors that arise when individuals pursue goals, interact socially, 
and respond to the affordances of their environment. 
Such behaviors need planning and coordination and are rarely modeled in current systems, 
which leads to repetitive dynamics that lack realism and adaptability.
Existing data-driven approaches depend on costly real-world crowd annotations and generalize poorly, 
while directly using LLMs to script crowd scenarios scales poorly, produces repetitive behaviors, 
requires heavy prompt engineering, and lacks structured outputs.

## Core Idea

Generative Crowds (Gen-C) is a framework which automatically generates high-level behaviors of virtual agents, 
enabling multi-character simulations with diverse crowds.<br>
Gen-C introduces:<br>  1. A graph-based representation that captures temporal  agent-agent and agent-environment interactions. <br>
2. A text-conditioned dual VGAE that jointly learns graph  structure and features, enabling scalable generation of novel multi-agent scenarios. <br>
3. A synthetic data pipeline, leveraging LLMs to bootstrap  crowd scenarios, reducing reliance on real annotations.

## Method

- **Input**: A natural-language scene description $S_{in}$, graph-level statistics (normalized counts of nodes, agents, and events,
  with maximum values of 100, 15, and 30, respectively), and a 13-dimensional action-frequency vector. These jointly form the condition vector $C$.

- **Representation**: A time-expanded crowd scenario graph $G=(V,E)$.
 Each node $V_i^t=(i,A_i^t,L_i^t)$ encodes an agent ID, an action (13 classes), and a location (8 classes).
  Edges are either **sequence edges**, connecting consecutive timesteps of the same agent, or **share edges**,
  connecting different agents performing a shared action at the same timestep.
  Each node has a 49-dimensional feature vector comprising learnable embeddings for action, location, and agent ID;
  a normalized sequence index; a 4D sinusoidal embedding of the global sequence index; and the top-4 eigenvectors of the normalized Laplacian.
  The symmetric adjacency matrix uses 0/1/−1 for no edge, sequence edge, and share edge, respectively.
  Nodes follow a canonical ordering by event index, then agent ID, then timestep.
  Text embeddings are obtained from the pretrained SentenceTransformer `all-mpnet-base-v2` (768D).

- **Model / Algorithm**: A dual conditional VGAE architecture with a shared GINE encoder
  (two layers, hidden dimension 128, residual connections, dropout 0.2).
  The encoder pools node embeddings, concatenates the graph-level vector with condition $C$,
  and outputs the mean and log-variance of a 32-dimensional latent variable.
  **VGAE-S** uses a three-layer MLP structure decoder with tanh outputs to reconstruct a symmetric adjacency matrix.
  **VGAE-F** uses a two-layer GINE feature decoder with two linear heads for actions (13 classes) and locations (8 classes),
  applying a collapse–predict–uncollapse strategy so that nodes connected by share edges within the same group receive consistent action
  and location predictions.
  A condition network projects the text embedding, scalar statistics, and action frequencies into 128 dimensions each,
  concatenates them, and produces a 32-dimensional condition vector $C$;
  two prior networks parameterize the conditional priors $p(Z_S\mid C)$ and $p(Z_F\mid C)$.
  Training maximizes the ELBO with SmoothL1 loss for structure reconstruction, cross-entropy loss for node features (action weight $\gamma=1.5$),
  and a KL term regularized by cyclical annealing ($\beta\in[0.001,3]$ for VGAE-S and $\beta\in[0.001,1]$ for VGAE-F).

- **Output**: New crowd scenario subgraphs generated by sampling $Z_S\sim p(Z_S\mid C)$ and $Z_F\sim p(Z_F\mid C)$
  and decoding them into an adjacency matrix and node features. From each decoded graph, the framework extracts per-agent action sequences,
  location sequences, and interactions. The environment layout can be provided manually or synthesized with a lightweight LLM query,
  and the resulting scenarios are instantiated and visualized as multi-agent crowd simulations in Unity.

## Crowd Simulation Aspect

- **Individual behaviour**: Each agent is represented as a time-expanded sequence of nodes encoding its action, location, and agent ID at every timestep.
  Individual behavior is therefore a temporally ordered sequence of high-level actions such as waiting, sitting, wandering,
  queuing, entering/exiting, reading, looking at, carrying, or talking on the phone, together with the environment location where each action occurs.
- **Group behaviour**: Agents that share an action at the same time are linked by share edges, merging their individual chains into group subgraphs.
  Gen-C generates coherent multi-agent interactions such as discussing, meeting, waving at, and queueing,
  and enforces consistency within a group via the collapse–predict–uncollapse strategy,
  so that interacting agents receive a common action and location at the same timestep.
- **Emergent behaviour**: Generated crowds exhibit heterogeneous action distributions, coherent agent–agent and agent–environment interactions,
  and high-level decision-making patterns consistent with real-world crowd dynamics.
- **Scalability**:  The method can handle any number of agents or groups because each generated graph describes one group,
  and many groups can be combined into a larger crowd.
  It was trained on about 22.9k subgraphs from the University Campus dataset and 25.1k from the Train Station dataset.
  At test time, it creates new scenarios by sampling from the learned distributions.
  LLMs are used only to make seed data, so the method avoids the poor scaling and repetition of asking an LLM to script whole crowds directly.

## Strengths

Structured high-level representation: Introduces a time-expanded crowd scenario graph that encodes actions, agent–agent interactions, 
and agent–environment context over time, filling a gap left by low-level navigation and local-diversity methods.

Text-conditioned dual VGAE: Jointly learns graph structure and node features through two synergistic VGAEs with a shared condition network, 
enabling scalable generation of novel multi-agent scenarios from natural language.

LLM-based synthetic data pipeline: Uses LLMs only to bootstrap a small seed set of crowd scenarios and converts them into graph data,
reducing reliance on costly real-world annotation 
while avoiding the poor scaling, repetitiveness, heavy prompt engineering, and unstructured outputs of direct LLM scripting.

## Limitations

LLM temporal imprecision (paper): 
LLMs lack precise temporal detail, so event sequences are derived from initiation constraints 
and action durations are sampled from action-specific distributions rather than generated directly.

Scope limited to high-level planning (paper): 
Gen-C generates structured high-level behavior graphs and is positioned to complement, not replace, low-level steering and collision-avoidance methods; 
it does not itself produce motion trajectories or physical dynamics.

Fixed discrete vocabulary (own): 
Actions are restricted to 13 labels and locations to 8 categories, which may limit expressiveness and prevent modeling behaviors outside the predefined set.

Limited scenario coverage (own): Evaluation covers only two themes, University Campus and Train Station, 
each with 500 generated scenarios, so generalization to other environments, cultures, or crowd types is unknown.

Q2： What limitations exist in rule-based and data-driven approaches? <br>
The paper argues that low-level methods fail to capture goal-directed, social, 
and environment-aware behaviors such as stopping to talk, browsing shop windows, or queuing for tickets. 
It also notes that data-driven methods depend on costly real-world trajectory collection and annotation and generalize poorly, 
while direct LLM scripting scales badly, repeats behavior, and needs heavy prompt engineering.<br>
Q3 — How can LLMs and generative models provide semantic-level control? <br>
Gen-C conditions a dual VGAE on natural-language scene descriptions plus graph-level statistics and action frequencies,
using LLMs to bootstrap synthetic scenarios and a condition network to align generated graphs with textual input. 
It shows how semantic-level control can be achieved through structured graph generation rather than free-form LLM output.
